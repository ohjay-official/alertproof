How a payment gets sealed.

Six steps, from the price to the trader’s own wallet. Every screen below is the real app on the public test mint.

Step 1

The trader types the price.

In naira or CFA, on a keypad laid out like a POS terminal. The app turns it into sats using a rate it saved while online, and always shows how old that rate is.

Step 2

A pay code appears.

It holds the shop’s name, the amount and the trader’s public key. Nothing secret. Making it needs no data, so the trader’s phone can stay offline all day.

Step 3

The customer pays from their own wallet.

Their camera opens Sealpay’s pay page in the browser. No new app, no sign-up. They pay a normal Lightning invoice from the wallet they already use. The page checks that the naira price on the code matches the sats it asks for, so a swapped code can’t hide a bigger amount.

Step 4

The mint seals the payment.

Once the invoice is paid, the mint issues the money as Cashu ecash and signs it. Two things make it a seal rather than a message:

  • A signature anyone can check offline. The mint attaches a proof (Cashu’s NUT-12) that it really signed this exact amount. Change one number and the proof breaks.
  • A lock to the trader. The money is locked to the trader’s key (NUT-11). The customer can’t spend it elsewhere or take it back.

The customer’s page shows the receipt as a QR code that plays in parts, so a cheap camera can read it.

Step 5

The trader scans it. No data needed.

The trader’s phone saved the mint’s public keys while it was online. It checks the seal against them, checks the lock is to its own key, and checks it has never seen this receipt before. Then the whole screen turns green, and the phone says “Paid, 2000 naira” out loud.

A second scan of the same receipt says Already used. A receipt from another mint, an edited amount or one locked to someone else says Not paid.

Step 6

Collect and cash out, later.

When the trader has data, one button collects the day’s payments: the mint swaps them for fresh money, still locked to the trader. Cashing out pays a Lightning invoice from the trader’s own wallet, which can be one that pays out in naira or CFA.

What it can’t promise.

Honest limits, stated where they matter.

  • The mint holds the money until the trader cashes out. A mint that fails could lose it, so keep small amounts there and cash out often.
  • The customer’s phone needs data to pay. It’s the trader’s phone that needs none.
  • If two phones share one shop, each only knows the receipts it scanned itself.

Why Cashu.

Lightning can’t pay a phone that’s offline. Cashu ecash can: the proof of payment travels with the receipt, so the trader’s phone can check it on the spot. The closest product, the Cashu team’s Numo, is the mirror image: its customer can be offline but its merchant must be online. Sealpay works when the trader isn’t.
